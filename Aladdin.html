Aladdin

Year: 1992

My Rating: ★★★★★ (5/5)

Aladdin. To be honest, I have nothing bad to say about Aladdin. It’s one of those rare animated films that just gets everything right. The story is strong, the pacing works, and the characters are memorable without ever feeling forced or exaggerated.

At its core, Aladdin is a story about identity, freedom, and love told in a way that’s both accessible for kids and meaningful for adults. Aladdin’s journey from street thief to someone who learns to be true to himself is well developed, and the message holds up even today. It’s about more than just magic carpets and genies. It’s about finding your place and being honest about who you are.

The soundtrack is one of the film’s biggest strengths. Songs like “A Whole New World”, “Friend Like Me”, and “Prince Ali” are not only catchy, they actually serve the story and deepen the emotion behind the scenes. There’s no filler here, each musical moment has weight and energy.

Visually, the animation still holds up decades later. The art style, colors, and setting all feel vibrant and immersive, making Agrabah a world you want to step into. And of course, Robin Williams as the Genie is a performance that’s impossible to ignore. He brought such life and personality to the character that it became one of the most iconic roles in animation history.

The love story between Aladdin and Jasmine also feels sincere. It’s not just a typical Disney romance, it has charm, struggle, and real emotion behind it. You care about them as individuals, not just as a couple, which is rare in animated films.

All in all, Aladdin is one of Disney’s strongest entries from the 90s. It's entertaining, heartfelt, beautifully made, and still worth watching today, whether you're revisiting it for nostalgia or discovering it for the first time.

Final score. 5 out of 5. A timeless classic that truly earns its place in animation history.
