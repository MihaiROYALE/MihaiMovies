Dumb and Dumber

Year: 1994

My Rating: ★★★☆☆ (3/5)

Dumb and Dumber is exactly what the title promises, dumb humor delivered by two incredibly clueless characters who somehow manage to hold your attention for the entire film. Whether that’s a good thing or not depends entirely on your sense of humor.

Jim Carrey and Jeff Daniels fully commit to the roles, and that’s really what makes the movie work. The stupidity isn’t half baked, they go all in, and it’s so absurd that at times it’s actually funny because it’s so over the top. Some scenes are iconic, no question the tuxedos, the briefcase, the “most annoying sound in the world” those moments stick with you, even if the rest of the movie doesn’t always hit the mark.

That said, this isn’t a smart comedy, and it doesn’t pretend to be. The plot is paper thin, the characters are borderline cartoonish, and a lot of the jokes rely purely on shock value or awkwardness. If you're in the mood for something mindless that makes you laugh at how ridiculous it is, this movie delivers. If you’re looking for anything deeper than that, this isn’t the place.

What saves it from being a total throwaway is how committed the performances are. Carrey is at his peak here, and even though Jeff Daniels was known for more serious roles before this, he somehow holds his own in this bizarre world. There’s also a strange kind of charm in how pure-hearted the characters are, even if they’re total idiots.

Final score. 3 out of 5. Not for everyone, and definitely not a “smart” comedy, but if you’re in the right mood, Dumb and Dumber delivers exactly what it promises, loud, low brow laughs with just enough charm to make it watchable.
